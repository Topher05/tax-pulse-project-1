# SYN-PB-01 Advisor Playbook for Estimated Payment Recommendations

This document is synthetic training content from the fictional Northstar Synthetic Tax Planning Authority. All rates, thresholds, dates, and limits in this document are invented and must not be treated as real tax law.

## SYN-PB-01-a Intake questions for non-withheld income

Ask whether the client expects dividends, consulting receipts, self-employment revenue, business distributions, investment sales, or other income not covered by withholding. Record the expected timing and whether the amount is recurring or one-time.

The goal is to separate source identification from payment calculation. If the advisor cannot name the income source, the recommendation is not ready for rule citation. If the source is known but the amount is uncertain, the advisor may document a range and rerun the projection later.

### SYN-PB-01-b Projection handoff

Before recommending estimated payments, compare the answer to SYN-TC-4100-a and the filing-status threshold in SYN-TC-4110-a or SYN-TC-4110-b. Use the rule citation in the technical note and use this playbook citation for advisor procedure.

The handoff should include the client profile, expected income quarter, filing status, and whether any income is seasonal. Seasonal facts may require a second rule citation to SYN-TC-4120-a.

## SYN-PB-01-c Recommendation wording

Use plain language: the client has income outside regular withholding, so the advisor is reviewing whether quarterly estimated payments reduce underpayment risk. Do not state that the synthetic provision is real tax law.

Avoid saying that the client "must" make a payment unless the recommendation has been reviewed. The playbook supports a cautious explanation: the advisor is evaluating a payment because non-withheld income can create a planning gap.

### SYN-PB-01-d Review escalation

Escalate to reviewer approval when projected non-withheld income exceeds 75,000 or when the client has both self-employment income and capital gain income in the same quarter.

Reviewer approval should focus on whether the selected rule citation matches the facts. A general trigger citation is not enough when the recommendation depends on filing status or seasonal income.
